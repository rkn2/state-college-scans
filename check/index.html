Sanborn Building Check

Each historic building is matched to the Sanborn fire insurance sheets that draw it (1906, 1911, 1922, 1929). For each of your buildings, confirm any sheet number the index wasn't sure of, then record what each sheet shows about the building. You can do a building in pieces. Everything saves as you go.

How to do one
  • Open on the map. The public map opens on the building with that year's Sanborn map underneath, outlined in red. Read the house number the Sanborn map prints on it, which is often different from today's.
  • Open the sheet. The library's viewer has a zoom. Find the same building by its streets and printed number. Sheet 1 of each year has the key map and street index.
  • Confirm the sheet (only for some buildings). Right sheet if the sheet draws this lot. Wrong sheet if it's on another sheet, and type the right number. Not on any sheet if that year's maps stop short of the lot. Not sure if you can't tell, and say why. A lot cut by a sheet edge is on both sheets, like 6 & 7.
  • What the sheet shows. For each year, the color gives the material (yellow frame, pink brick, blue stone, gray iron or metal, brown fireproof). The number in a corner is the stories (½ for a half story). The letter is the use (D dwelling, F flats, S store, A auto or garage). Copy the printed house number. Compare with the year before to say whether it changed. Use Can't tell or Other with a note when the sheet isn't clear.
